# Mening GIS Portfoliom

📍 Section 1: Study Area and Geographic SettingThe success of any geospatial analysis depends on a clearly defined study area. For this research, the Syrdarya Region of Uzbekistan was selected due to its unique transition from a traditional agricultural hub to a rapidly urbanizing industrial zone.Figure 1. Administrative boundary of Syrdarya region and detailed urban layout of Gulistan city.🌍 Geographic OverviewSyrdarya region is situated in the central part of Uzbekistan, on the left bank of the Syr Darya River. It serves as a vital corridor connecting the Fergana Valley with the rest of the country.Total Area: Approximately 4,280 $km^2$.Administrative Structure: The region consists of 11 administrative districts (Akaltyn, Bayaut, Gulistan, Mirzaabad, Sayhunabad, Sardoba, Syrdarya, Khavast, Boyovut, and others) and the regional center—Gulistan city.Significance: The region's economy is predominantly based on agriculture (cotton, grain, and melons), but the recent years (2017–2024) have shown a significant shift toward industrialization.🛠️ GIS Methodology for Site CharacterizationThe map above was developed using ArcGIS Pro to establish the spatial baseline for the LULC (Land Use and Land Cover) analysis.Vector Data Integration: Administrative boundaries were sourced from the National Cadastre Agency database to ensure legal and spatial accuracy.Coordinate System: To maintain geometric integrity and minimize distortion during area calculations, the WGS 1984 UTM Zone 42N projection was applied.Visualization: The central inset map focuses on Gulistan city, providing a high-resolution view of the urban fabric. This serves as the "Ground Truth" reference for identifying the "Built Area" class during the satellite imagery classification process.Cartographic Design: Professional symbology was used to distinguish between administrative borders, major transportation networks, and residential clusters, allowing for a clear interpretation of the regional infrastructure.🔬 Why Syrdarya? (Research Motivation)According to the article, the Syrdarya region represents a critical "socio-ecological laboratory." The presence of the Syr Darya river provides complex hydrological dynamics, while the proximity to Tashkent (the capital) drives socioeconomic pressure on land resources. This map establishes the spatial "Stage" where we analyze how every hectare of land has transformed over the last 8 years.
